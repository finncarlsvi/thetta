## Summary
This WoodWeb knowledge-base discussion gives practical guidance for small-part retention where vacuum performance is marginal. It compares thick tabs versus onion-skinning and outlines shop-floor tactics for stable cutting.

The resource emphasizes that tabs provide strong locking but increase cleanup, while onion skinning can reduce finishing effort through staged passes. It also discusses sequencing and setup details (sheet flatness, unused spoilboard coverage, tool selection) that materially affect whether tabs are even needed.

For research, this source contributes operational heuristics that can become rules in an automatic planner. In particular, it supports adaptive strategy selection based on part size, vacuum risk, and finishing tolerance rather than one static tab policy.

## Key Findings
- Thick tabs can strongly retain small parts, but at the cost of additional sanding/cleanup.
- Two-pass onion-skin approaches and vacuum-optimization tactics are practical alternatives for instability-prone layouts.

## Technical Details
The discussion suggests decision variables beyond geometry: vacuum zone efficiency, sheet warpage, pass order, and cutter type. A robust tab planner could integrate these as machine/material context inputs, not just contour-derived metrics.
