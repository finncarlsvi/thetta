# RES-024: When to cut a common line (The Fabricator)

## From Search Results
- Common cutting: two adjacent parts share a common edge, single cut separates them
- Minimizes material waste and reduces total cuts
- Decreases tool wear and cutting time
- Microtabs with common-line cutting adds process stability
- Tabbed parts stay in nest until end of punching operation, snapped out
- If common-line cut compromises edge quality or tolerance, probably not a good idea
- Thermal cutting: heat expansion can shift parts, causing errors
- Never let kerf from one piece overlap kerf from previous piece
- Unexpected machine shutdowns present challenges for restart
